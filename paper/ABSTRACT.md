# Extended abstract

**Status:** Manuscript in preparation (unpublished). Theoretical/computational design study; no device has been fabricated.

We propose and quantitatively model a magic-angle twisted-bilayer-graphene (TBG) Josephson junction embedded in a sub-terahertz silicon photonic-crystal cavity. We identify a measurable signature of its skewed current–phase relation (CPR): fractional (half-integer) Shapiro steps under microwave drive. A Bistritzer–MacDonald calculation places the flat-band minimum at 1.025°, with a converged flat-band width of 2.92 meV and a remote-band gap 40.7 times the drive-photon energy. The Beenakker CPR at transparency τ = 0.8 (c₂/c₁ = −0.187) drives a half-integer step of reduced width 0.200 I_c at the dome centre, four times the sinusoidal control floor. At I_c = 300 nA and R_n = 200 Ω this is 60 nA.

We use a floor-free step-width estimator with a sinusoidal-CPR control. The half-step retains 58.5 % of its width at Γ = k_BT/E_J = 0.014 (100 mK), and 50 % survival occurs at Γ* = 0.0180 (E_J/k_BT > 55.5, T_e < 129 mK). The zero-temperature filling signature (reduced width rising toward the dome edge while absolute width falls) inverts above ≈ 50 mK. We replace it with a collapse protocol: survival collapses against Γ = k_BT/E_J(ν) but not against T (residual ratio 4.0). The same curve calibrates the junction as a gate-tunable, model-calibrated electron thermometer (13–715 mK, resolution 1.5–3.8 mK). Observing ≥ 50 % survival alone bounds T_e < 129 mK.

The tuned L3 cavity (a = 586.74 µm) places its line at 145.000 GHz in a 137.5–177.9 GHz gap, with in-situ Q_i = 1.46×10⁴ and V_mode = 0.331 a³. The power chain closes at 90 nW → 900 pW → α = 1.000. Two design constraints emerge:

- An engineered collection structure (d_eff ≈ 20–25 µm) is required. A bare stack gives Q_J = 1.26×10⁸ and only about 1 µV of drive.
- A parametric T_e ≈ 0.6 K risk remains the principal thermal concern.

A companion boundary study finds that the step probes transparency rather than cleanly discriminating intrinsic from extrinsic origins. It also finds that the 145 GHz drive sits at or above the pair-breaking edge.
